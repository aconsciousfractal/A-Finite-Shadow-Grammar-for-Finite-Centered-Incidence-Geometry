# Public Claim Boundary

This document states what the paper and its reproducibility package do and do not establish. It applies to the current committed version of the repository.

## Interface claims

1. A concrete finite-shadow row is the five-slot datum `S=(N,X,I,rho,Phi)`, where `X` is a finite subset of `S_N` and `N` is the ambient permutation degree.
2. The identifiers `X01` through `X26` name source-family slots, not individual finite subsets.
3. The source-lock schema records provenance, action conventions, arithmetic or quotient data when triggered, verification, ownership, and import boundaries. This metadata is not a sixth mathematical slot.
4. Position-value balance is expressed through vanishing on the standard representation block in the stated permutation channel.
5. `Phi` is a descriptive, source-bounded fingerprint package. It is not a complete invariant, classifier, tiling oracle, or substitute for a separately proved decision theorem.

## Bounded exhibits

The package contains finite, source-aware exhibits for Type-A rows, selected signed Type-B/C rows, dihedral vertex shadows, the short/long partition of `G2`, projective `F4`, `H3`, `H4`, `D5`, `E6`, `E7`, and `E8` root shadows, a modular centered-operator row, and a controlled centered-rank census.

Each numerical route is limited to the explicit carrier, action, channel, and evidence named in the paper and route registry. A successful finite replay does not create a family-wide theorem.

The Type-A exact certificate supports the displayed Fourier/Specht ranks, weighted rank masses, block matrices, and lattice-qualified Smith values. It is checked by a read-only 61-check program and is separate from the twelve quantitative routes.

The signed mirror-union balance proposition is proved in the manuscript for all `n`; the accompanying numerical replay remains limited to the six displayed finite rows. Neither result is a classification of signed Type-B/C rows.

The quotient discussion records a classical source-side splitting test and a coupling obstruction. It makes no novelty claim for quotient existence, spectral inheritance, interlacing, or the general theory of extensions.

## Companion-work ownership

Companion papers remain independent sources. This paper may cite a definition, finite certificate, or bounded comparison without importing the companion theorem or proof. The public bibliography gives titles and immutable repository references for every companion result used as authority.

In particular:

- the two exceptional six-element packages retain their own theorem and certificate boundaries;
- the signed-reversal theorem remains in its dedicated public paper;
- the length-coloring, golden-channel, exceptional-root-shadow, and `E7` placement papers remain separate refinements;
- the higher-star defect and common-marked-lattice papers concern design incidence lattices and do not become new grammar rows here.

## Explicit nonclaims

The paper does not claim:

- a universal theory of finite centered incidence geometry;
- a general Coxeter, Weyl, or Type-E theorem;
- a reflection-subgroup or root-subsystem classification;
- a classifier, complete invariant, or tiling criterion derived from `Phi`;
- a general Sudoku or Latin-square mechanism;
- a second hidden group for the complete selected Latin-square rank locus;
- a general modular Specht theorem;
- a new quotient, descent, or transfer theory;
- an identified morphism behind the numerical value `5568`.

## Evidence rule

Every quantitative public claim must expose:

```text
claim -> source -> immutable version -> artifact -> command -> expected result -> SHA-256
```

Every proof-only or static-certificate claim must expose:

```text
claim -> definitions and sources -> fixed model -> proof or certificate -> independent check
```

If neither chain exists, the statement is outside the current claim boundary.
