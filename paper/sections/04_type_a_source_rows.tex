\section{Type-A Source Rows}

The Type-A source row is anchored by the published standard-space paper\cite{BabanskyyTypeAFCIG2026}.  Its one-incidence layer \(I_n\) is the first mature FCIG row in the grammar.  The public Type-A poset-cone and extension-tiler paper supplies the separate chamber/tiler context for rows X01--X02\cite{BabanskyyTypeAPoset2026}.  The underlying dictionary between poset cones and chamber unions of the braid arrangement is classical; poset cones and their Whitney-number refinements are developed in~\cite{DorpalenBarryKimReiner2021}, and~\cite{AguiarMahajan2017} treats the faces and cones of real hyperplane arrangements in general.  These are context citations only; no theorem from them is imported.  This paper uses these definitions and boundaries by citation; it does not merge their theorem claims.

In the notation of \cref{prop:standard-block-balance}, the Type-A balance theorem says that for even \(n\geq 4\), the one-incidence layer has
\[
  E_{I_n}=N_{I_n}-\frac{|I_n|}{n}J_n=0,
\]
so the standard block \(M_{(n-1,1)}(I_n)\) vanishes.  This is not merely vocabulary: it is the exact point where the centered-operator language and the Specht block language meet.

A second Type-A support row shows why \(\PhiFCIG\) cannot be reduced to a conjugacy-class histogram.  The Type-A value witness gives two explicit subsets of \(S_4\), in one-line notation,
\[
  X_A=\{2314,2341,2431,4231\},\qquad
  X_B=\{2413,2431,4213,4231\}.
\]
They have the same class histogram \((4):1,(3,1):2,(2,1,1):1\), but the \((3,1)\) block separates them.

For this witness the integral model is explicitly
\[
  L_{(3,1)}^{A_3}
  =\mathbb Z(e_1-e_4)\oplus\mathbb Z(e_2-e_4)
   \oplus\mathbb Z(e_3-e_4),
\]
with \(D(w)(e_i-e_4)=e_{w(i)}-e_{w(4)}\) and matrices acting on columns.
Thus every Smith form in the following table is qualified by this displayed
lattice.  The static certificate records the two block matrices,

It is used here as context for how finite rows enter the grammar, not as a license to broaden the Type-A theorem boundary. The syntheme-transversal result and the certified six-element classification remain companion-owned routes\cite{BabanskyyS6Syntheme2026,BabanskyyS6TileClassification2026}; this paper imports neither theorem nor proof and makes no all-\(n\) tiling or classification claim.
