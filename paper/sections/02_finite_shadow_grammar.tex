\section{The Finite-Shadow Grammar}

\begin{definition}[finite-shadow grammar row]
A finite-shadow grammar row is a tuple
\[
  S=(n,X,I,\rho,\PhiFCIG).
\]
Here \(n\) records the ambient symmetric degree, \(X\subseteq S_n\) is the finite row under study, \(I\) is the incidence or selection rule, \(\rho\) is the representation or centered-operator channel, and \(\PhiFCIG\) is the recorded fingerprint package.
\end{definition}

For signed rows the ambient object may be a source-locked finite shadow \(H\leq S_N\), with \(X\subseteq H\).  The signed Type-B/C row used later has this form with \(B_n\leq S_{2n}\), following the signed-shadow conventions used in the companion Type-B/C artifact.

\begin{definition}[admission levels]
The admission levels are governance labels, not new mathematical structures.  The active labels are: \emph{core}, \emph{core artifact}, \emph{core exhibit}, \emph{prose}, \emph{pointer only}, \emph{parked}, \emph{future}, and \emph{rejected}.  Their role is to prevent an unfinished or overbroad object from entering the manuscript as if it were a closed theorem source.
\end{definition}

The full instance matrix is recorded in \cref{app:source-manifest}.  The table is part of the grammar: it says not only what enters, but also what remains outside.  This is why continuous contact sketches, raw motion rows, the parked exceptional \(S_6\) benchmark, and the separate signed-reversal route are not silently absorbed into the present paper.

\begin{remark}[the teeth test]
A proposed FCIG row fails the teeth test if it lacks any of the five slots.  A geometric picture without a finite \(S_n\) shadow, a finite set without an incidence rule, or a representation calculation without a source-locked finite object can still be useful research material, but it is not a core row of this paper.
\end{remark}
