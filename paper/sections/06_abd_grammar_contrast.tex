\section{A/B/D Grammar Contrast}

The A/B/D material enters this paper as grammar contrast.  Its purpose is to show how the same admission discipline reacts when the signed setting changes the row shape.  The signed-shadow routing table and signed-balance notes are the source locks for this contrast.

The contrast is deliberately asymmetric.  The Type-A row has a published theorem source, the Type-B/C row has a closed bounded artifact plus the signed mirror-union source row of \cref{prop:signed-mirror-union-balance}, and the D-side signed reversal material belongs to the separate signed-reversal route.  This paper may point to that route, but it does not import theorem statements from it.

In this sense the grammar has teeth: it can admit a bounded signed finite shadow while refusing to smuggle in a neighboring theorem route whose source locks are not yet closed.  It also shows a real change of behavior.  The Type-A row balances through one centered channel and is parity-fragile on the odd side; the signed row must balance two channels at once, but the mirror-union does so for every \(n\geq3\).
